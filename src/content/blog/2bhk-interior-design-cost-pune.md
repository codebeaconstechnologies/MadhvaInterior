"How much will my 2 BHK interiors cost?" is the first question almost every homeowner asks us — and the honest answer is that two flats of exactly the same size can end up with very different budgets. The size of the flat matters far less than **what you include** and **what it is made of**.

This guide walks through the decisions that move the number, so that when you sit down with any designer in Pune you know what you are comparing.

## 1. Scope: what's actually included

The biggest driver of cost is simply how much work is in the quote. It helps to think of 2 BHK interiors in three layers:

- **Essentials** — a modular kitchen and wardrobes in both bedrooms. Most families start here, because these are the things you cannot live without.
- **The finished look** — a TV unit with storage, a false ceiling with cove and accent lighting, shoe and utility storage at the entrance, and a dressing or study unit.
- **Complete styling** — wall panelling, feature walls, loose furniture (sofa, bed, dining table), curtains, décor and lighting fixtures.

Two quotes that look far apart are often just covering different layers. Before you compare prices, compare the item lists.

## 2. The board inside your furniture

Wardrobes, kitchen cabinets and TV units are built on a "carcass" — the box you don't see. The common options are plywood (in different grades), MDF and HDHMR board. They differ in strength, screw-holding, moisture resistance and price.

In a Pune flat, moisture is the deciding factor for kitchens and anything near a bathroom. The cabinet under your kitchen sink needs a moisture-resistant material with sealed edges; a bedroom wardrobe can use a different grade without any loss in life. A good quote specifies the material **for each area**, not one material for the whole home. We explain the trade-offs in detail in [Plywood vs MDF for Home Interiors](/blog/plywood-vs-mdf-home-interiors).

## 3. The finish on the outside

The shutter finish is what you see every day, and the price range across finishes is wide:

- **Laminate** — durable, easy to clean, huge range of colours and textures; the most common choice.
- **Acrylic and high-gloss finishes** — a mirror-like look, popular in kitchens; costs more and needs more careful cleaning.
- **Veneer** — real wood on the surface; warm and premium, needs polishing.
- **Painted (PU/duco) finishes** — seamless, any colour; among the more expensive options.

Many homes mix finishes: a premium finish on the TV wall that guests see, and a practical laminate inside wardrobes.

## 4. Hardware

Hinges, drawer channels, lift-up fittings, wardrobe sliding systems and kitchen accessories (cutlery trays, pull-outs, corner units) are a large and often under-estimated part of the budget. They also decide how your furniture feels after five years. Soft-close hinges and good drawer channels are worth prioritising over decorative extras.

## 5. False ceiling and electrical work

A false ceiling across the living room and bedrooms, with cove lighting and profile lights, changes how a 2 BHK feels more than almost anything else — but it also adds material, labour and electrical points. Whether you do a full ceiling, a peripheral ceiling or only a feature section in the living room is a real budget lever. Our [false ceiling guide](/blog/false-ceiling-design-cost-guide) covers the options.

## 6. The costs people forget

When you plan your budget, keep room for items that are not always in an interior quote:

- Your housing society's interior-work deposit or charges, if any
- Repairs to the builder's work — seepage, hollow tiles, window sealing
- Electrical changes and additional points
- Painting after carpentry is complete
- Appliances: chimney, hob, water purifier, washing machine
- Curtains, light fixtures and décor
- Deep cleaning before you move in
- Applicable taxes

## How to compare 2 BHK interior quotes

Ask every designer for an **itemised quote** that names, for each item: the size, the carcass material and grade, the shutter finish, and the hardware. Then compare line by line. A lower total that quietly uses a lower-grade board in the kitchen isn't cheaper — it's a different product.

It's also worth asking how payments are linked to progress, what the warranty covers, and who supervises the site day to day.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I do my 2 BHK interiors in stages?
Yes. Many families do the kitchen and wardrobes first and add the TV unit, false ceiling or loose furniture later. Designing the whole home first keeps later stages consistent with the first.

### Is a cheaper quote always worse?
Not always — but check that it includes the same items, the same board grade in wet areas, the same finishes and the same hardware. Differences in those details explain most gaps between quotes.

### Why don't you publish a fixed price per square foot?
Because the same square footage can hold very different scopes and materials. An itemised quote based on your actual design is the only number you can rely on.
