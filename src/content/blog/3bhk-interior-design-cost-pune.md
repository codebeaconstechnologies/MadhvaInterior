A 3 BHK isn't simply a 2 BHK with one more room. The third bedroom usually brings a third wardrobe, often a study setup, a larger living and dining area, and sometimes a second bathroom vanity to plan. More rooms means more decisions — and more places where a material choice multiplies across the home.

Here is how to think about the cost of 3 BHK interiors in Pune, and how to keep it under control.

## Where a 3 BHK budget goes

In most homes, the bulk of the interior budget sits in built-in carpentry:

- **Wardrobes** — three bedrooms usually means three sets of wardrobes, often full height with lofts. Because they're large, the board and finish you choose for wardrobes has a big effect on the total.
- **The kitchen** — a 3 BHK household often cooks more and stores more, so the kitchen tends to be larger, with more tall units and accessories.
- **Living and dining** — TV unit, crockery or bar unit, a console at the entrance, and possibly wall panelling.
- **The third bedroom** — a kids' room with a study desk, a guest room, or a home office. What it becomes decides what it costs.

On top of carpentry come the false ceiling and lighting, any electrical changes, painting, and — if you want a finished, styled home — loose furniture, curtains and décor.

## Decide what the third bedroom is for

This one decision shapes a surprising amount of the budget. A home office needs a proper desk, cable management and good task lighting. A kids' room needs durable finishes, a study surface and storage that will still work in five years. A guest room might only need a comfortable bed and a modest wardrobe. If the room has to do two jobs, a wall-mounted desk with a sofa-cum-bed or storage bed can let it — but plan it on paper first.

## Spend where it's used, save where it isn't

The most useful budgeting rule for a 3 BHK is to match material quality to how hard each area works:

- **Wet and hard-working areas** — the kitchen, especially under the sink, and anything near a bathroom — need moisture-resistant board and sealed edges. This is not the place to save.
- **Visible feature areas** — the TV wall, the entrance — are where a premium finish is noticed every day.
- **Inside wardrobes** and in a guest room that's used a few times a year, a practical laminate finish performs perfectly well.

## Phasing a 3 BHK

Many families don't do everything at once, and that's a sensible way to manage a larger budget. A common sequence:

1. **Phase one:** kitchen, all wardrobes, and the electrical and false ceiling work — anything that is messy, dusty or built into walls.
2. **Phase two:** TV unit, crockery unit, study furniture and panelling.
3. **Phase three:** loose furniture, curtains, lighting fixtures and décor.

The key is to **design the whole home before starting phase one**. That way the false ceiling has the right light points for furniture you'll add later, and every phase uses the same family of materials and colours.

## Costs outside the interior quote

As with any new flat, keep money aside for society charges or deposits, repairs to the builder's work, appliances, painting, curtains, deep cleaning and taxes. These add up faster in a larger home.

## Reading a 3 BHK quote

With more rooms, it's easy for a quote to become a long list that's hard to compare. Ask for it to be organised **room by room**, with each item's size, carcass material, finish and hardware named. Then you can see, for example, exactly what changing the finish on three wardrobes would save.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 3 BHK much more expensive than a 2 BHK?
It usually costs more because there is more carpentry — especially wardrobes — but the difference depends far more on scope and materials than on the extra room itself.

### What should we do first if the budget is tight?
Start with the kitchen, wardrobes, electrical work and false ceiling, since these are hard to add once you have moved in. Furniture and décor can follow.

### Can every bedroom have a different style?
Yes, and it often should — but keep shared elements like the flooring, the door finish and the lighting style consistent so the home still feels cohesive.
