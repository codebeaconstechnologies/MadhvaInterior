A well-designed false ceiling changes the feel of a room more than almost any other single item. It hides wiring, creates places for soft cove and accent lighting, and can make a plain room look finished. A badly planned one lowers the room, cracks at the joints, or leaves lights in the wrong places.

Here's what to know before you design one.

## Types of false ceiling

**Gypsum board** is the most common choice in Indian homes today. Boards are fixed to a metal frame, jointed, and finished with paint. Gypsum is quick to install, gives a smooth finish, and has good fire resistance. In humid areas such as bathrooms, use moisture-resistant boards or a different material.

**POP (plaster of Paris)** is applied by hand on a frame. It allows very free-form curves and detailing, but it's slower, messier and depends heavily on the skill of the person doing it.

**PVC panels** are light, water-resistant and termite-proof, which makes them useful in bathrooms, balconies and utility areas. They have a more utilitarian look than gypsum.

**Wood and wooden slats** add warmth and texture, often as a feature over a dining table, a TV wall or a bed. They're usually combined with a gypsum ceiling rather than covering the whole room.

## Common designs

- **Peripheral (border) ceiling** — a band around the edge of the room, keeping the full height in the centre. Ideal for rooms with modest ceiling height.
- **Full ceiling** — the whole room is lowered, with lights recessed throughout.
- **Cove ceiling** — a recess that hides an LED strip, washing the ceiling with soft indirect light.
- **Feature ceiling** — a section over the dining table, bed or living area, sometimes with wood or a different finish.
- **Profile lights** — slim linear lights set flush into the ceiling for a clean, modern look.

## Ceiling height matters

Every false ceiling lowers the room where it runs. In flats with modest floor-to-ceiling heights, a peripheral design keeps the centre of the room at full height so it doesn't feel compressed. Check where beams, AC units and curtain tracks will go before fixing the design.

## Plan the lighting with the ceiling

The ceiling and the lighting plan are really one decision. Before the boards go up, fix:

- Where each light goes, and what it lights (the sofa, the dining table, the counter)
- Which lights are on which switch, and whether any should be dimmable
- AC piping and fan positions
- Curtain pelmets and tracks
- Access panels where you may need to reach wiring later

Wiring and AC piping must be complete before the ceiling is closed.

## What decides the cost of a false ceiling

- **Area covered** — full ceiling vs peripheral vs a single feature
- **Material** — gypsum, POP, PVC or wood
- **Design complexity** — multiple levels, curves and coves add labour
- **Lighting** — the number of lights, cove strips, profile lights and drivers
- **Electrical work** — new points and wiring inside the ceiling
- **Finishing** — putty, primer and paint

Because lighting can be a large part of the total, ask for the ceiling and the lighting to be listed separately in your quote.

## Avoiding cracks

Hairline cracks along board joints are the most common complaint with false ceilings. They're reduced by a properly levelled frame, correct jointing tape and compound, and enough time for the joints to settle before painting. Ask how joints are treated.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a false ceiling necessary?
No, but it's the easiest way to add layered lighting and hide wiring. A peripheral ceiling or a single feature section can give much of the effect at a lower cost.

### Gypsum or POP — which is better?
Gypsum is faster, cleaner and more consistent, which is why it's the more common choice. POP allows more free-form detailing but depends more on the craftsman.

### Can I install a false ceiling in the bathroom?
Yes, using moisture-resistant boards or PVC, with good ventilation.
