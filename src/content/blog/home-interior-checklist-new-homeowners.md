Home interiors involve hundreds of small decisions, and it's the forgotten ones — a missing plug point, a wardrobe without a loft, no light over the kitchen counter — that you notice every day afterwards. Use this checklist to make sure nothing important slips through. It works whether you're doing the whole home or a few rooms.

## Before the design

- [ ] Builder's snag list done: seepage, hollow tiles, window sealing, drainage, electrical points
- [ ] Society rules for interior work: working hours, deposits, NOC, lift use, debris disposal
- [ ] Floor plan with accurate measurements
- [ ] A list of who lives in the home and how each room will be used
- [ ] An honest list of what you need to store (clothes, utensils, luggage, documents, appliances)
- [ ] A budget, and a split between "before move-in" and "can wait"
- [ ] Your move-in or griha pravesh date, if fixed

## During the design

**Living room**

- [ ] Sofa size and position decided before the TV wall is designed
- [ ] TV unit with closed storage, and a plan for set-top box, router and wires
- [ ] Plug points for TV, lamps, chargers and a vacuum
- [ ] Ceiling design and lighting: ambient, cove and accent

**Kitchen**

- [ ] Layout around the platform, gas line and sink
- [ ] Places for chimney, hob, microwave, water purifier, fridge and mixer
- [ ] Enough plug points along the counter
- [ ] Drawers vs shelves decided for each base unit
- [ ] Under-cabinet lighting
- [ ] Moisture-resistant material for the sink cabinet

**Bedrooms**

- [ ] Wardrobe internals planned: long hanging, short hanging, drawers, lofts, locker
- [ ] Sliding vs hinged doors decided for each room
- [ ] Bedside switches and charging points
- [ ] Study or dressing unit, if needed
- [ ] Reading light and a softer night light

**Entrance and utility**

- [ ] Shoe storage with ventilation
- [ ] A spot for keys, bags and umbrellas
- [ ] Washing machine point, drying space and storage for cleaning supplies

**Bathrooms**

- [ ] Vanity storage in water-resistant material
- [ ] Exhaust fan and ventilation
- [ ] Mirror lighting

## Materials and finishes

- [ ] Board type and grade written down for each area (see [Plywood vs MDF](/blog/plywood-vs-mdf-home-interiors))
- [ ] Shutter finish chosen for each room
- [ ] Hardware specified: hinges, channels, sliding systems, handles
- [ ] Edge banding on all exposed edges
- [ ] Paint colours and finish
- [ ] Samples seen in person, in daylight

## The quote

- [ ] Itemised, room by room, with sizes, materials, finishes and hardware
- [ ] Clear about what's excluded (appliances, painting, civil work, loose furniture)
- [ ] Payment schedule linked to progress
- [ ] Warranty terms in writing
- [ ] A written schedule

## During execution

- [ ] Electrical and plumbing changes completed before the false ceiling
- [ ] Material grades checked on site against the quote
- [ ] Regular site updates from one point of contact
- [ ] Changes agreed in writing before they're made

## Handover

- [ ] Walk through every room and test every door, drawer, light and switch
- [ ] Snag list made and closed
- [ ] Warranty details and care instructions received
- [ ] Deep cleaning done before move-in

## Frequently asked questions

### What is most often forgotten in home interiors?
Plug points and lighting — especially along the kitchen counter, beside beds and behind the TV. They're cheap to add during design and messy to add later.

### Should I buy furniture before or after the interiors?
Decide the sizes of major furniture — sofa, bed, dining table — during design, so the built-in work fits around them. You can buy them later.
