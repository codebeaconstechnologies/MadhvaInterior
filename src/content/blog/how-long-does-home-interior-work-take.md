"When can we move in?" is the question every family asks, often with a griha pravesh date already in mind. The honest answer is that the timeline depends far more on **scope and decisions** than on the size of the flat. A 2 BHK with only a kitchen and wardrobes is a very different project from a 2 BHK with panelling, a full false ceiling and custom furniture in every room.

Rather than quote a number that wouldn't fit your home, here's what each stage involves and what makes it faster or slower — so you can plan a realistic schedule with your designer.

## Stage 1: Design

This is where your home is decided on paper:

1. Understanding how you live and what you need
2. Layouts for every room
3. 3D visualization, and revisions
4. Material and finish selection
5. Final drawings and an itemised quote

**What speeds it up:** a clear brief, quick feedback on each round, and having the decision-makers available together.

**What slows it down:** changing the brief midway, waiting for family members to review, and many rounds of revisions on finishes.

Time spent here is rarely wasted. Every decision made on screen is one that doesn't hold up the site later.

## Stage 2: Materials and manufacturing

Once the design and quote are approved, materials are ordered. If furniture is factory-made, production starts; if it's made on site, the carpenters need the materials delivered.

**What speeds it up:** choosing finishes and hardware that are readily available.

**What slows it down:** special-order finishes or imported hardware, and changes to the design after production has started.

## Stage 3: Site work

Site work follows a fixed order, because each trade depends on the one before:

1. Civil repairs and any changes
2. Electrical wiring, new points and AC piping
3. Plumbing changes
4. False ceiling
5. Carpentry: kitchen, wardrobes, TV unit and storage
6. Painting
7. Lights, fittings and hardware adjustments

**What speeds it up:** an empty flat, a society that allows reasonable working hours, and decisions already made.

**What slows it down:** restricted working hours, waiting on the builder to fix defects, rework from late design changes, and humid weather slowing down paint and polish.

## Stage 4: Handover

The final walkthrough, a snag list, fixing the snags, and deep cleaning. Plan a little time for this — rushing it to meet a move-in date is how small defects become permanent.

## What really decides the timeline

- **Scope** — the number of rooms and items, and how complex each one is
- **Decision speed** — the biggest factor you control
- **Factory-made vs site-made furniture**
- **Society rules** on working hours and material movement
- **The flat's condition** — seepage or builder defects that must be fixed first
- **Season** — monsoon humidity slows drying and curing
- **How many projects your designer is running** — a team spread across many sites moves slower on each one

## Planning around a griha pravesh date

If you have a fixed date, tell your designer at the very first meeting. Work backwards from it: decide what must be complete for the ceremony (often the kitchen and the pooja area), and what can follow. It's better to finish the essentials properly than to rush everything.

## Ask for a written schedule

Any designer should be able to give you a stage-by-stage schedule along with the quote, showing what happens when and which decisions are needed from you by which date. Review it together, and update it if the scope changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why can't you give a fixed timeline for a 2 BHK?
Because two 2 BHKs can have very different scopes, materials and site conditions. We give a schedule for your project once the design and scope are clear.

### What's the most common cause of delay?
Late changes to the design after work has started. Finalising the design in 3D before execution avoids most of them.

### Can work continue during the monsoon?
Yes, but drying and curing take longer in humid weather, so painting and polishing stages may need more time.
