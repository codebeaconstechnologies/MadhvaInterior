A modular kitchen is usually the single most expensive piece of carpentry in a home — and the one that takes the most punishment. Heat, steam, water, oil and dozens of openings a day will find any weakness in materials or hardware. That's why kitchen quotes vary so much, and why the cheapest option is rarely the cheapest over ten years.

Here's what goes into the cost of a modular kitchen in Pune.

## Layout and size

Most flats in Pune are handed over with a kitchen platform already built — usually L-shaped or parallel, often with a granite top and a sink. The cabinets are designed around that platform, so its shape and length set the starting size of your kitchen.

On top of the base cabinets, the layout decides how many **wall units** (overhead cabinets) and **tall units** (floor-to-ceiling storage for appliances, a pantry or a fridge surround) you have. Tall units add a lot of storage, and a lot of cost — but in a compact kitchen they're often the best value for money per litre of storage.

If you're considering changing the builder's platform — to a U-shape, or adding a breakfast counter — that involves civil work and new countertops, and should be decided early.

## The carcass: what the cabinets are made of

The box of each cabinet is the carcass. In a kitchen, moisture resistance matters more than anywhere else in the home:

- **Under the sink**, water will eventually reach the cabinet. This area needs moisture-resistant board with every edge sealed.
- **Near the hob**, heat and steam are constant.
- **Wall units** stay drier and can sometimes use a different material.

Ask what board is used **in which part** of the kitchen. Our [plywood vs MDF guide](/blog/plywood-vs-mdf-home-interiors) explains the options.

## The shutter finish

Shutters are what you see and touch, and they drive a large share of the price difference between kitchens:

- **Laminate** — hard-wearing, easy to wipe down, available in matte, textured and gloss; the practical default.
- **Acrylic** — glossy and vivid; looks striking but shows fingerprints and fine scratches more.
- **PU / lacquer** — smooth, seamless and available in any colour; premium, and needs a careful finish to last.
- **Veneer** — real wood grain; warm and natural, but needs more care around water and heat.

A matte laminate in a deep colour can look every bit as considered as a premium finish — the [Walnut Residence](/gallery/walnut-residence) kitchen pairs matte green cabinetry with walnut-toned wall units.

## Hardware: the part that wears

Hinges and drawer channels open and close thousands of times a year. Soft-close hinges, full-extension drawer channels and good lift-up mechanisms for overhead units are what make a kitchen feel good after years of use. Ask what hardware is included, how many drawers versus shelves you're getting, and what the warranty covers.

Drawers usually cost more than shelves, but in base cabinets they're far easier to use — you pull the contents out to you instead of kneeling to reach the back.

## Accessories

Cutlery and thali trays, bottle pull-outs, corner carousels or "magic corners", tall pantry pull-outs, plate racks, waste-bin pull-outs and detergent holders are all optional, and they add up quickly. Choose them for how you actually cook. A family that stores a lot of steel utensils needs a different set of accessories from one that mostly cooks one-pot meals.

## Appliances and services

Decide early whether the chimney, hob, oven, microwave and dishwasher are part of the kitchen quote or bought separately, and plan where each one goes. Just as important are the services behind them:

- **Piped gas** — many Pune societies have piped gas lines and meters in the kitchen. Cabinets must be planned around the pipe and meter, with access left for maintenance.
- **Electrical points** for every appliance, plus under-cabinet lighting so you're not working in your own shadow.
- **Chimney duct** route to the outside.
- **Water purifier** location and plumbing.

These cost little to plan and a lot to fix after the cabinets are in.

## Where to spend and where to save

- **Spend on:** moisture-resistant board in wet zones, sealed edges, good hinges and drawer channels.
- **Choose carefully:** shutter finish — pick for how you clean, not just how it looks on day one.
- **Save on:** decorative extras and accessories you won't use daily.

## Getting an accurate kitchen quote

Ask for a quote that lists every cabinet with its size, the carcass material, the shutter finish, the hardware, and each accessory. Compare quotes line by line, not by the total.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to replace the builder's granite platform?
No. If it's in good condition, the cabinets can be designed around it. Replace it only if you want a different layout or countertop.

### Is a modular kitchen better than one made on site by a carpenter?
Both can work well. What matters most is the material grade, the edge sealing, the hardware and the quality of installation. Ask any supplier about all four.

### Which finish is easiest to maintain?
Matte or textured laminates are generally the most forgiving: they hide fingerprints and are easy to wipe clean.
