Getting the keys to a new flat is exciting — and then the questions start. What should be done first? When should you call a designer? What happens to the false ceiling if the electrician hasn't finished? Most expensive mistakes in interiors come from doing things in the wrong order.

This is the sequence we recommend to homeowners moving into a newly handed-over flat in Pune.

## Step 1: Check the flat before anything else

Before any interior work begins, walk through the flat carefully and list anything the builder needs to fix. Once wardrobes and panelling cover the walls, these problems become much harder to see and repair.

- **Seepage and damp patches**, especially on external walls, around windows and next to bathrooms
- **Hollow-sounding tiles** — tap them; hollow tiles can crack later
- **Window and balcony door sealing** — check during or right after rain if you can
- **Bathroom slopes and drainage**
- **Electrical points** — do they all work, and are they where you need them?
- **Doors and locks**

Raise these with the builder in writing and get them fixed first.

## Step 2: Understand your society's rules

Most housing societies have rules for interior work. Find out early:

- Permitted working hours and days
- Whether a deposit or NOC is needed
- Rules for using the lift to move material
- Where debris has to go
- Whether any changes to the balcony or exterior are allowed

Your designer will need to plan the work schedule around these.

## Step 3: Decide your priorities and budget

Write down what you need, not just what you like. How many people will live here? Who works from home? How much do you cook? What do you need to store — and what can you get rid of before moving? Then set a budget, and decide what must be done before you move in versus what can come later. Our guides to [2 BHK](/blog/2bhk-interior-design-cost-pune) and [3 BHK](/blog/3bhk-interior-design-cost-pune) budgets explain what moves the cost.

## Step 4: Get the design done before work starts

This is the step people are most tempted to skip, and the one that saves the most money. A proper design fixes:

- The furniture layout in every room
- Where every electrical point, switch and light goes
- The false ceiling design and lighting plan
- Kitchen and wardrobe layouts, inside and out
- Materials and finishes

Seeing the design in **3D** before execution lets you catch problems on screen — a wardrobe door that hits the bed, a TV wall that's too close to the sofa — instead of on site.

## Step 5: Electrical and plumbing changes come first

Once the design is fixed, any electrical rewiring, new points, AC piping and plumbing changes are done first. These go inside walls and ceilings, so they must be finished before the false ceiling closes up and before carpentry covers the walls.

## Step 6: False ceiling, then carpentry

The false ceiling usually comes next, followed by the carpentry — wardrobes, kitchen, TV unit and storage. Whether furniture is made in a factory or on site changes how this stage runs, but the order stays the same.

## Step 7: Painting, lights and finishing

Painting is done after the dusty work is over. Then come light fixtures, switch plates, hardware adjustments and any wallpaper or panelling.

## Step 8: Snag list and deep cleaning

Before you move in, walk through every room with your designer and list anything that needs adjusting — a door that doesn't close cleanly, a scratch, a misaligned shutter. Once those are fixed, get the home deep-cleaned.

## A note on timing in Pune

- **Monsoon:** paint and polish take longer to cure in humid weather, and seepage problems show up clearly in the rain — a good time to check for them, a slower time to finish surfaces.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I contact an interior designer?
As soon as you have possession — or even before, if you have the floor plan. The earlier the design is done, the better electrical and ceiling work can be planned.

### Can I move in and do the interiors later?
You can, but dusty and noisy work like the false ceiling and carpentry is much easier in an empty flat. Many families do the essentials before moving in and add the rest later.

### Do I need to fix the builder's defects before interior work?
Yes. Seepage, hollow tiles and leaking windows should be repaired first; they're very hard to fix once interiors cover them.
