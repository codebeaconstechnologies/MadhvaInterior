Almost everything built into a modern Indian home — wardrobes, kitchen cabinets, TV units, beds with storage — sits on a board you never see once the finish goes on. The choice of that board decides how long your furniture lasts, how well it holds screws and hinges, and how it copes with humidity.

The question we hear most is "plywood or MDF?" The real answer is **both, in the right places**.

## What plywood is

Plywood is made of thin layers of wood veneer glued together, with the grain of each layer running across the one below. That cross-layering makes it strong, stable and very good at holding screws — which matters for anything with hinges, channels and heavy loads.

Plywood comes in different grades, broadly by how well the glue resists moisture:

- **MR (moisture resistant)** — suitable for dry interior areas such as bedroom wardrobes.
- **BWR (boiling water resistant)** — better moisture resistance, commonly chosen for kitchens.
- **BWP / marine** — the highest water resistance, for the wettest areas.

Look for the grade and the ISI mark printed on the sheet, and ask for the brand and grade to be written into your quote.

## What MDF is

MDF (medium density fibreboard) is made from wood fibres bonded with resin under heat and pressure. It has no grain and a very smooth, uniform surface. That makes it excellent for:

- **Painted and PU finishes**, which need a perfectly smooth base
- **Routed and grooved designs** — fluted panels, CNC patterns, curved profiles
- **Decorative panels and shutters**

Its weaknesses: standard MDF swells if water gets into it, and it doesn't hold screws as firmly as plywood, especially if they're removed and refitted. Moisture-resistant MDF grades exist and perform better.

## What HDHMR is

HDHMR (high density, high moisture resistance) board is a denser fibreboard engineered for better moisture resistance and screw-holding than standard MDF. It's widely used for shutters and, increasingly, for carcasses. Like any material, its performance depends on the brand and on proper edge sealing.

## Which to use where

| Area | What matters | Typical choice |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen base cabinets, especially under the sink | Water and steam resistance | Moisture-resistant board (BWR/BWP plywood or a suitable HDHMR) with sealed edges |
| Kitchen wall units | Moderate humidity, load from crockery | Plywood or HDHMR |
| Bedroom wardrobes | Strength, screw-holding for hinges | Plywood (MR or better) |
| Bathroom vanity | Direct water exposure | Water-resistant board, or materials like PVC |
| Painted or PU shutters | Smooth surface | MDF or HDHMR |
| Fluted, grooved or CNC panels | Clean machining | MDF |
| TV unit carcass | Strength, long spans | Plywood |

## Why Pune's climate matters

Pune's monsoon brings months of high humidity, and kitchens and bathrooms add steam on top. Boards fail at their **edges** first: if an edge is left unsealed, moisture creeps in and the board swells or delaminates. Whatever board you choose, insist on proper edge banding on every exposed edge — including the hidden ones inside cabinets.

Termites are another concern in many homes. Ask whether the board is treated, and make sure cabinets aren't fixed directly against a damp wall.

## Lifespan

Material life depends on quality, grade and how well it's installed and maintained. As a general guide, quality plywood used correctly can last a couple of decades or more, and MDF lasts well in dry areas where it's protected from water. ## What to check in your quote

- The board type **and grade** for each area, not one line for the whole home
- The brand, if you have a preference
- The thickness used for carcasses, shelves and back panels
- Edge banding on all exposed edges
- The shutter material and finish, listed separately from the carcass

## Frequently asked questions

### Is MDF bad for furniture?
No. MDF is excellent for painted finishes, grooved designs and decorative panels. It's just not the right choice where it will get wet or where hinges need very strong screw-holding.

### Should my entire kitchen be marine plywood?
Not necessarily. The wettest zones benefit most from the highest water resistance; other parts of the kitchen can use a suitable moisture-resistant grade. Your designer should specify by area.

### Can I see the material before it's used?
You should be able to. Ask to see the board, its grade marking and the edge banding before production starts.
